# Mutual NDA Risk Review

| Severity | Count |

**Attorney Review Recommended: YES** — Two RED items carry meaningful financial

---

## Provision-by-Provision Analysis

### § 1 — Definition of Confidential Information

**GREEN** | Confidence: High

Standard broad definition covering non-public business and technical
information. Mutual scope is appropriate. No carve-outs listed (e.g., publicly
available info, independently developed, received from third parties) — absence
of standard exclusions is a minor gap but common in short-form NDAs.

_Suggested redline:_ Add standard exclusions (public domain, independent
development, third-party disclosure without restriction, compelled disclosure
with notice).

---

### § 2 — Standard of Care

**GREEN** | Confidence: High

"Same degree of care as its own, not less than reasonable care" is the
industry-standard formulation. Bilateral. No issues.

---

### § 3 — Survival Period (5 years)

**YELLOW** | Confidence: Medium

Five years is on the longer end for a mutual NDA (industry norm is 2–3 years).
For trade secrets, 5 years may still underprotect; for routine business info, it
may overcommit resources. The clock starts at _disclosure_, not termination — so
obligations can run well past the business relationship.

_Suggested redline:_ Confirm 5 years is acceptable for your longest-lived
confidential disclosures. Consider a shorter period for non-sensitive
categories.

---

### § 4 — No IP License

**GREEN** | Confidence: High

Standard protective clause. No issues.

---

### § 5 — Termination & Survival

**YELLOW** | Confidence: Medium

30-day written notice termination is standard. However, the agreement doesn't
specify what happens to materials (return or destroy) upon termination. This
creates ambiguity about ongoing use of previously disclosed information.

_Suggested redline:_ Add a return-or-destroy clause triggered on termination,
with a certification requirement.

---

### § 6 — Governing Law & Arbitration

**RED** | Confidence: High

Binding arbitration in Wilmington, DE waives your right to a jury trial and
limits class/joint actions. If you are not Delaware-based, this imposes travel
cost and unfamiliar jurisdiction. Arbitration can be slower and more expensive
than small-claims court for low-value disputes.

_Suggested redline:_ Negotiate for mutual choice of law / venue (e.g., each
party's home state for disputes it initiates), or at minimum remove "binding"
arbitration in favor of mediation-first with litigation fallback. Confirm with
counsel.

---

### § 7 — Liability Cap ($10,000)

**RED** | Confidence: High

The $10,000 total liability cap is dangerously low for most commercial
relationships. If confidential information is disclosed in breach — e.g., a
product roadmap, customer list, or pricing model — actual damages could far
exceed $10,000. The indirect/consequential damages exclusion compounds this:
lost profits, lost business opportunities, and reputational harm are all
excluded.

_Suggested redline:_ Remove or substantially raise the cap (common alternatives:
cap at fees paid under a related agreement, or exclude IP/confidentiality
breaches from the cap entirely). At minimum, carve out willful misconduct and
gross negligence from the damages exclusion.

---

### § 8 — Entire Agreement / Amendments in Writing

**YELLOW** | Confidence: High

Standard integration and amendment clause. Acceptable. Minor note: no
severability clause present — if any provision is found unenforceable, the
agreement may fail as a whole rather than severing only that provision.

_Suggested redline:_ Add a standard severability clause.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Sign with eyes open on the liability cap.** $10,000 is a very low ceiling —
   if your disclosed information has meaningful value, this cap leaves you
   underprotected against breach.
2. **Arbitration in Delaware** may not be your preferred forum — negotiate if
   possible.
3. **Missing boilerplate** (no-exclusions list, no return-or-destroy, no
   severability) is common in short NDAs but worth patching before long-term
   use.
4. **Overall:** Reasonable mutual NDA for low-stakes exchanges. For anything
   involving trade secrets, product IP, or a high-value relationship, engage
   counsel before signing.
